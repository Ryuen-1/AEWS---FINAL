The primary objective of this capstone project is to develop an AI -powered 
Academic Early Warning System (AEWS) utilizing predictive analytics to identify at -risk 
students in Philippine SUCs, enabling proactive interventions that enhance student retention 
and institutional performance. Specifically, this study aims to:  
1. Analyze multi -source institutional data from College of Technologies at Bukidnon State 
University for feature extraction and as inputs to the predictive model.  
2. Design a role-based secure web system compliant with data privacy laws and aligned with 
CHED SAS and AMU access guidelines.  
3. Develop a role-based web system that detects and provides early warning analytics using 
XGBoost prediction model. 
4. Test accuracy of the AI model using standard metrics such as precision, recall, F1 -score, 
and ROC-AUC. 
5. Evaluate the software’s functionality, usability, and reliability.

1.4 Scope and Delimitations. 
The present capstone project includes the design, development, and testing of a working 
Academic Early Warning System (AEWS) for Bukidnon State University. The system focuses on 
identifying students who may need early academic assistance and on simplifying the Academic 
Mentoring Unit (AMU) referral procedure. It is implemented as a role -based web application for 
administrators, instructors, AMU staff, and students.  
The AEWS encompasses several primary features designed to support role -based 
workflows. Functional capabilities include user authentication and account access control, class 
management, student enrollment management, grade and attendance tracking, AI -assisted risk 
prediction using an XGBoost classification model, automatic and manual referral generation, digital 
needs-assessment form handling, intervention and support routing, in -system and email 
notifications, activity logging, search and filtering, archiv e management, and role -specific 
dashboards and reporting. 
The pilot study focuses on Bukidnon State University. The data used by the implemented 
system consists of institutional and user -managed academic records available through the AEWS 
workflow, including class records, student enrollment data, gradesheets, attendance records, referral

3.1.3 Data 
The data used for model development consisted of institutional and system -managed 
academic records from Bukidnon State University, including attendance records, gradesheet data, 
and needs-assessment responses. These datasets provided the basis for extracti ng the deployed 
model features, including previous GPA, failed subject count, attendance rate, academic challenge 
score, external factor score, midterm grade, class standing, laboratory grade, and major output 
grade. The final model -ready dataset contained  1,000 student records after cleaning, matching, 
normalization, and label preparation.

3.2 Methods 
This chapter describes the systematic strategy to be used in the design, development and 
evaluation of the AI-based Academic Early Warning System (AEWS) for Bukidnon State University. 
It gives details on the materials, software, and hardware configuration that will be used, followed by 
the research design and development process model for project execution. It goes on to explain the 
process of data collection, preprocessing, machine learning model development, system integration, 
as well as detailed evaluation strategies. The methods are designed to meet the specific goals of the 
study to ensure that there is a robust and user -centered system that will ensure accurate risk 
prediction and early academic interventions. Through an iterative process of agile development and 
rigorous testing this chapter provides a clear framework for developing a scalable, reliable, and 
effective AEWS solution.  
3.2.1 Research Design 
This study will follow a Design Science Research (DSR) paradigm that is widely used in 
information systems literature in order to build and evaluate innovative artifacts for solving identified 
organizational problems. The process will follow the establishe d DSR process model by Peffers et 
al, which consists of six activities which are sequentially but iterative: Problem Identification and 
Motivation, Definition of Objectives for a Solution, Design and Development, Demonstration, 
Evaluation and Communication. Our main artifact is the integrated platform of AEWS software, and

the utility of our software will be assessed in its authentic organizational context, the student support 
framework of Bukidnon State University.  
The method of the research would be of mixed type, with a combination of both quantitative 
and qualitative methods at various stages. A quasi -experimental component will be incorporated in 
the evaluation phase to compare retention and performance measures of a cohort of students 
exposed to the AEWS-driven interventions as compared with a similar historical control group from 
past academic years. Simultaneously, a qualitative case study component will include in-depth, semi-
structured interviews and focus gr oup discussions with faculty advisors and AMU staff post -
implementation to probe themes of usability, integrating workflow, and perceived effectiveness. This 
approach is consistent with the philosophy of pragmatism which emphasizes practical solutions and 
values both objective measures and subjective experiential knowledge in order to fully understand 
the impact of the artifact.  
Ethical considerations will be brought to the front throughout the design. Beyond complying 
with data privacy laws we will conduct what is called algorithmic impact assessments to proactively 
review the potential risks of bias or unfair exclusion in the da ta predictive model. We will also put in 
place a human -in-the-loop protocol, by which all AI -generated risk flags will be reviewed by a 
designated staff member to ensure that any type of formal intervention is not triggered unless a 
human safety net is in place, thereby keeping humans in the loop and accountable for the 
consequential decisions made that impact students.

3.2.3 Requirements Gathering and Analysis 
The requirements engineering phase will employ a multi -modal strategy to capture both 
explicit needs and latent requirements. We will conduct approximately 15 semi-structured interviews 
(lasting 45-60 minutes each) with a purposively sampled group of stakeholders. An interview protocol 
will guide discussions around current monitoring practices, pain points in identifying at-risk students, 
data accessibility, and desired reporting features.

Needs-assessment responses were used to identify academic and external factors that may 
affect student performance. Individual responses were analyzed and summarized into two grouped 
indicators: academic challenge score and external factor score. The acade mic challenge score 
represented learning-related concerns, such as difficulty understanding lectures, weak study habits, 
low motivation, and difficulty with specific subjects. Meanwhile, the external factor score represented 
non-academic concerns, including financial difficulties, health -related concerns, family issues, part -
time work, and mental health -related concerns. These indicators allowed the system to consider 
circumstances beyond grades and attendance when assessing student risk.  
Before model development, the consolidated dataset underwent data profiling to examine its 
structure, completeness, validity, and consistency. The data requirements included attendance rate, 
midterm grade, final grade, previous GPA, failed -subject count, and needs-assessment indicators. 
Attendance values were verified to fall within the valid range of 0 to 100 percent, while grade-related 
fields were checked for consistency with the BukSU grading scale of 1.00 to 5.00. The datasets were 
also checked for mis sing values, duplicate records, and inconsistencies after integration through 
Student_ID. 
The profiling results showed that the prepared training dataset contained 1,000 student 
records. After validation and preprocessing, no missing values remained in the model-ready dataset. 
The class distribution consisted of 410 Low Risk records and 590 Hig h Risk records. These results 
indicate that the dataset was complete, valid, and suitable for feature extraction and student -risk 
prediction.

The predictive performance of the XGBoost model will be measured with the help of 
precision, recall and F1-score values. These metrics will be calculated based on the confusion matrix 
which will be obtained by comparing the actual student academic outcomes  to the model's 
predictions in the test dataset. Recall will measure the effectiveness of the system in determining 
which students will be in actual danger and precision the accuracy of the system's alerts about the 
students being in danger and the F1-score a way of measuring the predictive ability of the system as 
a whole. These metrics will enable the study to objectively determine whether the machine learning 
model will be effective in achieving the target of early and reliable identification of the academic risk.
